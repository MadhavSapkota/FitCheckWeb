Haircuts for a Heart-Shaped Face: What Works, What to Watch and What to Ask For

By FitCheck Team · Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026 · 10 min read

The best haircuts for a heart-shaped face add softness or width around the jaw and soften the forehead. Think chin-length bobs, lobs, layers that start at the chin, side-swept fringes and curtain bangs. Be careful with heavy blunt fringes and lots of volume on top, which can stress the width of the forehead.

What is a heart-shaped face, and how do I know if I have one?

A heart-shaped face is widest across the forehead and cheekbones and narrows to a smaller, often more pointed chin. It is a loose styling category, not a diagnosis, and many faces sit between two shapes.

The proportions to look for

  • The forehead is the widest or one of the widest parts of your face.
  • The cheekbones are similar in width to the forehead or slightly narrower.
  • The jaw tapers toward a narrower chin, which may look soft or pointed.
  • A widow's peak (a downward point at the hairline) is common but not required.

A quick mirror or photo check

Pull your hair back and stand in natural light with the camera at eye level. Compare the width of your forehead, cheekbones and jaw. If the forehead and cheekbones are clearly wider than the jaw, a heart shape is a reasonable description. If the jaw is almost as wide, or the face is quite long, you may be a mix, and that is very common.

Treat this as a rough guide. Our broader guide on how to choose a haircut that suits you shows where face shape fits alongside hair type, lifestyle and upkeep.

What makes a haircut work on a heart-shaped face?

A flattering cut for this shape balances the proportions. It adds some fullness at the jaw and chin and keeps the forehead from looking broader. These are rules of thumb, not laws.

Add softness or width around the jaw and chin

Hair that ends around the chin, jaw or just below creates fullness where the face is narrowest. That is why chin-length bobs, lobs and layers that begin at the jaw are the usual starting points.

Soften the forehead instead of emphasizing it

Side-swept fringes, curtain bangs and face-framing pieces break up the width of the forehead. Soft, light edges tend to look more balanced than a hard horizontal line.

Keep the top from adding extra width or height

Big volume at the crown, combined with a narrow lower half, can make the top of the head feel wider than the chin. A flatter, smoother top with movement lower down usually looks more even.

Which short haircuts suit a heart-shaped face?

Short cuts that keep some length and softness around the face, such as a chin-length bob or a pixie with a longer side-swept top, tend to work best. The aim is to avoid exposing the full width of the forehead with nothing to soften it.

Chin-length bobs and soft bobs

A bob that ends at the chin or just below adds visual weight at the jaw. Slightly rounded or lightly softened ends look gentler than a razor-sharp line. A subtle side part adds to the balance.

Pixie cuts with a longer, side-swept top

If you want a pixie, ask for extra length on top that can sweep across the forehead. Wispy, piecey texture around the ears and nape keeps the look soft.

Short styles to approach carefully

Very short, tight cuts with the hair pushed straight back leave the forehead and cheekbones fully on show. They can look great, but if you want a more balanced effect, keep some fringe or side length.

Which medium-length haircuts suit a heart-shaped face?

Medium lengths are often the easiest fit. A lob or shoulder-length cut with movement at the ends puts fullness right where a heart-shaped face is narrower.

Lobs and shoulder-length cuts

A long bob that skims the collarbone or shoulders gives weight below the chin. Ask for softly finished ends instead of a heavy, hard line if you want movement.

Layers that begin around the chin or jaw

Layers that start at chin level add body to the lower half of the face. Layers that start high, near the cheekbones, can add volume where you already have width, so say where you want the shortest layer to land.

Textured ends and soft waves

Gentle waves or flicked-out ends at jaw level create fullness without much effort. A loose bend with a curling iron or overnight waves is enough.

Which long hairstyles suit a heart-shaped face?

Long hair works well when the volume sits below the cheekbones and a few pieces frame the face. Very long, straight, flat hair with no shaping can pull the eye down to the narrow chin.

Long layers that add fullness below the cheekbones

Long layers starting around the chin or lower give movement through the ends. Keep the top smooth and the layering lower for a balanced outline.

Side-swept or face-framing pieces

Face-framing strands that start at the cheekbone or chin and sweep outward soften the forehead and add width near the jaw.

Ponytails and updos

Loose, soft updos with a few pulled-out strands at the front are generally more forgiving than a slicked, tight ponytail that shows the full forehead and hairline. A tight style is not wrong, just a more graphic look.

Which fringes, bangs and parts suit a heart-shaped face?

Side-swept fringes and curtain bangs usually suit this shape well, because they cover part of the forehead and add softness. A deep side part also works well, though a center part can look good too.

Side-swept fringes and curtain bangs

Curtain bangs part in the middle and sweep to each side, which breaks up the forehead and flows into the cheekbones. A long side-swept fringe does the same job with a diagonal line. Both are fairly easy to grow out.

Wispy or soft fringes versus heavy blunt fringes

Wispy, see-through fringes let the forehead show through lightly. A thick, blunt, brow-grazing fringe creates a hard horizontal line across the widest part of the face. Some people love that effect, so it is a style choice rather than a mistake.

Deep side part versus center part

A deep side part adds asymmetry and softens the forehead. A center part is more symmetrical and shows more of the face, and it tends to look best with face-framing layers or curtain bangs. Try both in the mirror before deciding.

How do I adapt heart-shaped face haircuts to my hair type?

Hair type changes how the same cut behaves, so adapt the idea instead of copying a photo exactly. The principle stays the same: fullness near the jaw, softness at the forehead.

Straight and fine hair

A bob or lob that ends at or just below the chin, with ends only lightly softened, can keep fine hair looking fuller. Heavy texturizing and lots of layers can make thin ends look wispy, so keep both minimal.

Wavy hair

Waves naturally add width at the ends, which can suit this shape. A lob or medium cut with long layers lets the waves sit at jaw level. A side-swept fringe often blends in easily.

Curly and coily hair

Curls expand, so it helps to have length and shape judged on dry or styled curls. Watch the crown, where extra volume can add width on top. Our guide to hair styles for curly hair goes through cuts by length.

Thick hair and thin hair

With thick hair, ask your hairdresser to remove bulk higher up, around the crown and upper layers, so the top does not add extra width. Keep the ends and the area around the jaw full so the lower half still has weight. Thin hair often looks fuller with a chin-length cut, a softly finished edge and a soft fringe.

If you are worried about thinning, shedding or scalp problems, speak to a doctor or dermatologist. Haircut advice cannot assess or treat those concerns.

What are the best haircuts for men with a heart-shaped face?

Men with a heart-shaped face often do well with a textured top, some length at the front and a little weight at the jaw. The aim is to soften the forehead and avoid emphasizing a narrow lower face.

Textured crops and side-swept fringes

A textured crop with a slightly longer, side-swept fringe softens the forehead without needing much length. Ask for texture on top and avoid a hard, straight fringe line.

Medium-length styles

A natural, relaxed top with length that falls partly over the forehead, or a side part, balances the wider upper face.

Fades and tapers

Tapers are fine, but a very tight, high fade can make the sides look small next to a wide forehead and cheekbones. A longer taper that blends gradually is more balanced.

Stubble or a short beard

A short beard or stubble adds weight at the jaw and chin, which can balance a narrower lower face. Keep it neat and not too pointed.

Which styles should I be careful with on a heart-shaped face?

Be careful with styles that make the top of the face look wider or the chin look smaller.

  • Heavy, blunt, very short fringes create a strong line across the widest part of the face.
  • Lots of height and volume at the crown with tight sides can exaggerate a top-heavy outline.
  • Very short, tight styles with no fringe or side length show the forehead fully.

Careful does not mean never. If you love a certain cut, a hairdresser can adjust the length, texture or placement so it works better for you.

How much should face shape decide my haircut?

Face shape is one input, and often not the biggest one. Hair type, density, how much time you will spend styling, and your own taste usually matter more.

Hair type, density and upkeep

A cut that needs daily blow-drying is a poor choice if you air-dry. Pick something that works with your hair's natural texture.

Glasses, hairline and personal style

Frames sit across the forehead and cheekbones, so check fringes and side pieces with your glasses on. A strong widow's peak or cowlick can also change how a fringe sits.

Pick two or three cuts and test them

Instead of hunting for a single ideal cut, shortlist two or three and compare them side by side.

What should I tell my hairdresser or barber?

Bring photos and say what you like about each one. Clear details about length, layers and fringe work better than a cut name alone. For example: "I like the length in this photo, the curtain bangs in that one, and the softness at the ends." Also point out what you do not want.

Useful phrases

  • "Start the layers at my chin, not at my cheekbones."
  • "Keep the fringe long and wispy, so it sweeps to the side."
  • "I want fullness around my jaw, but not much volume on top."
  • "Softly finished ends instead of a hard line."

Questions about upkeep

Ask how long it will take to style, how often you will need a trim, and how it will look as it grows out.

Frequently asked questions

Do bangs suit a heart-shaped face?

Often yes, if they are soft. Curtain bangs and long side-swept fringes break up the width of the forehead. A thick, blunt, very short fringe can emphasize it, so start with wispy or longer options.

Is long or short hair better for a heart-shaped face?

Neither is better in itself. Where the fullness sits matters more than length. Chin-length to shoulder-length cuts add weight near the jaw, while long hair works with layers below the cheekbones and a softer front.

What is the best bob for a heart-shaped face?

A chin-length or slightly longer bob with softly finished ends is a common choice, because it adds fullness at the jaw. Pair it with a side part or a light fringe to soften the forehead.

Can men with heart-shaped faces wear short cuts?

Yes. A textured crop with a slightly longer side-swept front tends to look balanced. Very tight sides with a lot of height on top can emphasize the narrow chin, so a gradual taper is usually a safer choice.

Can I wear a center part?

Yes, a center part can work well, especially with face-framing layers or curtain bangs. If you feel it highlights your forehead, try a deep side part to soften the effect.
